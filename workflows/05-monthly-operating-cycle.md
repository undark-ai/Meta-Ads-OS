# Monthly operating cycle

Wider than the weekly read, narrower than a full audit. Run it as the standing monthly review.

## Sequence

| Step | § | What | Agents |
|---:|---:|---|---|
| 1 | 2 | Measurement re-check — has anything drifted? Dedup rate, EMQ, event freshness | 21–36 (abbreviated) |
| 2 | 3 | Reconciliation refresh: claim ratio, blended MER, modelled share | 37–42 |
| 3 | 5 | 30/90/365 and year-over-year, mapped onto the change log | 51–54, 148–151 |
| 4 | 7–8 | Creative database refresh; fatigue and evergreen verdicts | 59, 60, 65–70 |
| 5 | 9 | Angle learning — what changed since last month | 71–75 |
| 6 | 15 | Frequency and saturation | 100 |
| 7 | 16 | Catalog health, where the account runs one | 101–108 |
| 8 | 29 | Budget allocation for the coming month | 153–155 |
| 9 | 30 | Scorecard, against the same published weights as last month | 156 |

## Compare against the last run

The point of a monthly cycle is movement. Read the previous run's `scorecard.md` and
`coverage.md` — labelled as history, with their dates — and report what changed.

**Never change the scorecard weights between runs on the same account.** A score that moved
because the weights moved is not a result, and it destroys the only longitudinal signal the
account has.

## Seasonality first

Check the prior-year comparison before calling any movement a finding. A decline that matches
last year's shape at the same point is the market. An account flat year over year in a month it
usually grows has a problem no month-over-month view will show.

## Deliverables

The refreshed dashboard, the scorecard with its movement, the month's angle learning, and a
one-page read: what changed, what caused it, what to do about it, and what to watch.
