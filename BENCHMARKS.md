# Benchmarks — Meta DTC creative

External reference numbers for judging whether an account's creative operation is normal, healthy
or broken. **These are not thresholds this repository enforces.** No agent reads this file as a
rule. It exists so a finding can say *"hit rate is 4%, against a 10–15% healthy band"* instead of
*"hit rate is 4%"*, and so a recommendation can be argued against a number rather than a feeling.

**Provenance matters more than the numbers.** Every figure below is one practitioner's observed
range, not a measured industry study. Cite the source when you use one, and never present a range
here as though this repository measured it.

## Curtis Howland — Meta DTC creative, $150M+ spend

*Posted by Curtis Howland to LinkedIn on 2026-09-17. Self-reported, from his own account portfolio
and ~50 DTC brands he tracks. The original capture retained the date and author but not the post
permalink; do not cite the profile itself as though it were the source. Author:
https://www.linkedin.com/in/curtishowland/. Related long-form publication:
https://newsletter.curtishowland.com/p/ive-spent-100m-on-meta-ads-in-the.*

### Testing split

| band | share | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| Iterations | 50% | Take a proven winner, change one thing |
| Adjacent | 40% | Same selling angle, new format |
| Big swings | 10% | Net-new wildcard concepts |

### Creative churn

- **27–35% of spend** churns to new ads every month in high-volume accounts.
- You replace roughly a third of your winners just to stay flat.

### Hit rate

- **10–15%** of new ads clearing the CPA threshold is healthy.
- **70%** of creatives get cut before week 2.

### Spend concentration

- The top **1–2%** of ads drive around **50%** of spend and revenue.
- Concentration risk: if those top ads share one selling angle, they fatigue together.

### Kill / test / scale zones

| zone | share of ads | action |
|---|---|---|
| Scale | ~1% | Drive ~50% of revenue. Move budget onto them **20% at a time** |
| Test | 65%+ | Let them spend until they prove out or drop to kill |
| Kill | remainder | Enough spend to be **90% confident** the ad never hits target → off |

### Creative vs copy

- Creative is roughly **10x more impactful** than primary text.
- Copy still worth testing: two primary texts on the same creative measured **$350 CPA vs $200 CPA**.

### Testing budget

- Target **1 new concept per $10k** of monthly spend.
- Each new ad should get around **$3k** in spend to read.

### Hook rate

- Median across 50+ DTC brands: **32%**.
- Target **35%+**. Top quartile of brands run **42%+**.

### Winner lifespan

- Average winner lasts **45–60 days**. Top performers run **90+**.
- Keep the creative pipeline **30 days ahead**, so replacements are live before decline starts.

## How to use these

1. **As context in a finding, never as a pass/fail gate.** An account at 8% hit rate is below the
   band; that is a question to investigate, not a defect to report.
2. **Check the shape of the account first.** These are high-volume DTC numbers. A low-spend account
   or a considered-purchase B2B account will not match them, and should not be forced to.
3. **The concentration and lifespan numbers are the two that generalise best.** Top-1% dominance
   and 45–60 day decay show up nearly everywhere; the split percentages and the $3k read threshold
   are budget-dependent.
4. **Add sources, do not replace them.** When a second practitioner's numbers land, add a section.
   Conflicting ranges from two credible sources are information, not an error to resolve.
