# Reveals

Brainstorm well-known phrases first: sayings, idioms, and proverbs thematically connected to the product's world — its category, its benefits, or the problems it solves. These are your setups. Then complete each one with an unexpected ending that reframes, contradicts, or extends the original meaning in a way that says something true about the product. The setup primes the reader; the reveal is where the idea lands.

**Structure:** "[Familiar phrase or setup]... [unexpected ending that reframes it around the product]."

**Examples:**
- Gold's Gym: "Keep our beaches beautiful. (And pick up the trash, too.)"
- Cider Jack Hard Cider: "It's like mom used to make. Just before she was arrested."
- AppsFlyer: "All you need is love. But an MMP with 24/7 global support wouldn't hurt either."
- AppsFlyer: "Always follow your gut. (Except when allocating ad budgets. Then, follow your data.)"
- JFM 102.2 (radio): "Annoy the neighbours. Turn it down."
- Adidas: "Winning isn't everything. It's the only thing."
- Saab: "To crash with a Volvo is extremely safe. If you're sitting in a Saab."
- Roku: "Americans watch 5 hours of TV a day. We can do better!"
- Wild Turkey: "You can't hurry love or bourbon. Especially bourbon."
- The Economist: "Money talks, but sometimes it needs an interpreter."
- monday.com: "Rome wasn't built in a day... but your dream workflow can be."
- Ramp: "Time is money. Save both."
- Eurostar: "Time flies when you don't."
- The Weather Network: "Written, produced and directed by God."
- University of Oxford: "To be, or not to be? That is two questions."
- Nike: "'66 was a great year for English football. Eric was born."
- JFrog: "Imagine there's no version."
- SwissLife: "I never want children are great."
