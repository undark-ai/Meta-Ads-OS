# Popular (Batch)

Run 8 headline techniques together for a fast, varied batch of ~20 labeled headlines, about two or three per technique. Pick the strongest angle for each technique rather than forcing a weak one — variety and quality across the set matter more than hitting an exact count. Label each headline with its technique.

**The 8 techniques:**

1. **Antithesis** — put two opposites side by side so the contrast sells it.
   Example: Apple: "Small chip. Giant leap."

2. **Alliteration** — repeat the same starting sound across at least 3 words.
   Example: Canopy: "Build better B2B lending products."

3. **Metaphor** — say the product is something from a completely different world.
   Example: Claude: "A jetpack for your thoughts."

4. **Personification** — give the product or the problem human traits, feelings, or a voice.
   Example: Columbia: "This jacket is a climate denier."

5. **Reveals** — start with a cliché phrase or idiom, then twist the second half to land your point.
   Example: Cider Jack: "It's like mom used to make. Just before she was arrested."

6. **Self-Aware Ads** — openly admit it is an ad and wink at how advertising works.
   Example: Dublin Advertising Photography: "Headlines this dull need pictures."

7. **Tricky Threes** — list two normal things, then break the pattern with a funny third.
   Example: Postmates: "When you like your coffee without sugar, cream, or pants."

8. **Wake-Up Call** — alert the reader to a costly habit they don't realize they're in.
   Example: IBM: "Is your best employee working somewhere else?"
