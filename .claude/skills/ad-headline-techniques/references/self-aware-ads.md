# Self-Aware Ads

The headline openly admits it's an ad, names the game being played, or winks at the reader about how advertising works — and that honesty becomes the hook. People tune out ads, but they pay attention when an ad talks back to them. The self-awareness disarms cynicism and makes the brand feel human, which is the whole point.

**Examples:**
- Dublin Advertising Photography: "Headlines this dull need pictures."
- Posterscope: "You're living proof that posters get read."
- Oatly: "You actually read this? Total success."
- Oatly: "Another ad for our oat drink providing no reason at all why you should try it."
- Upwork: "This headline is 100% clickbait... but the high-quality talent on Upwork is real."
- Samsung: "This is an annoying ad for a tool that helps you remove annoying ads like this annoying ad from your photos."
- Chivas Regal: "Don't bother to read this ad. Just taste Chivas Regal."
- Project Research, Inc.: "If you don't like this ad, we'll find out why."
- Surreal: "Please buy our cereal."
- RXBAR: "Really big words."
- Oatly: "We made this poster so that you would find this to put in your coffee."
- Apple TV+: "This is what happens when the creatives and executives can't agree on a billboard idea."
- Travel Supermarket: "If we needed a headline explaining what we do, it would go here."
